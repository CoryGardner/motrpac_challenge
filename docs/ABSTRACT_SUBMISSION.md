Can a compact molecular signature identify a tissue reliably? In the MoTrPAC 6-month rat endurance-training transcriptomes (19 tissues, 899 vials, 50 animals) a 20-gene panel chosen by a class-aware round-robin selector reaches 0.976 balanced accuracy under animal-grouped cross-validation (k = 50: 0.993; all genes: 0.995); the same k with an F-test selector reaches 0.399. Reliability has two further parts. A conformal prediction set that promises the true tissue 90 % of the time covers 0.908 of held-out vials in-distribution, but with the same calibration only 0.618 of adult rat BodyMap organs from another laboratory, where the panel still names 1.000 of organs correctly, and 0.364 of human GTEx samples (accuracy 0.654); the loss is abstention (empty sets), not confident error. Three target animals restore coverage within species (0.943 at 1.00 tissues per set); across species they do not (0.954 at 11.7 tissues per set). Finally, within one multi-tissue study the tissue axis is confounded with processing: only 1 of 171 tissue pairs shares an RNA extraction plate, library batch and flowcell, and library QC numbers alone classify tissue at 0.975. Within-study accuracy is therefore not evidence of biology; transfer to an independently processed cohort is. Code and site: MIT.
