In the MoTrPAC 6-month rat endurance-training transcriptomes (19 tissues, 899 vials, 50 animals), a 20-gene panel selected inside each fold by a class-aware round-robin rule reaches 0.976 balanced accuracy under animal-grouped cross-validation (50 genes 0.993; all genes 0.995; an F-test selector at the same size 0.399). A split-conformal set promising the true tissue 90 % of the time covers 0.916 of held-out animals in-distribution, but with the same calibration only 0.618 of adult rat BodyMap organs from another laboratory, where the panel still names all mapped organs correctly (9 of 11; muscle and brain as super-classes), and 0.364 of human GTEx samples (accuracy 0.654); the loss is abstention, not confident error. Three target animals restore coverage within species (0.943 at 1.00 tissues per set); three donors do not across species (0.954 at 11.7 tissues per set). Within one multi-tissue study the tissue axis is confounded with processing: each tissue sits inside one extraction plate, library batch and flowcell, only 1 of 171 tissue pairs (ovary and testes) shares all three, and library QC numbers alone classify tissue at 0.975 (0.873 from purely technical ones). Within-study accuracy is therefore not evidence of biology; transfer to an independently processed cohort is.
